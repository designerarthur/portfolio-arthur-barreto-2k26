        magicfeet redesign: getting shoppers to the right shoe in fewer clicks

        magicfeet needed a store that gets people to the right shoe, in the right size, in fewer clicks. So we started with what shoppers do, not with how the page looked.

        01 · Decisions

        Four decisions, each with a reason

        1. A persistent sidebar puts size first, the facet that decides if a shoe is even an option. Shoppers narrow the list without losing their place.

        2. A four-column grid with a corrected hierarchy: image, name, then price. Badges and wishlist stay secondary, so cards are easy to scan and compare.

        3. The active item changes color and gains a left border, so people always know where they are.

        4. Category navigation starts from what people do, like running, football or training, in a minimalist layout with soft pastel tones from the brand.

            01

            Gender first, then type

            Parents start from who the shoe is for. Masculino and feminino lead the panel, and each category opens in place, so nobody leaves the menu to explore.

            • Hick’s Law
            • Progressive disclosure
            02

            One campaign, one anchor

            The Father’s Day card sits alone on the left. A single promo with a bold caption is the first thing the eye lands on, without competing with the navigation.

            • Von Restorff
            • Focal point
            03

            Brands you recognize, not read

            Logo tiles in a two-column grid make brands easy to scan. A tile next to a name is faster to spot than a plain text list.

            • Recognition over recall
            • Similarity
            04

            Mobile goes one level at a time

            Each screen has one job. A back button, a title and a breadcrumb say where you are, rows are 56px tall for easy taps, and “see all” stays pinned at the bottom, in thumb reach.

            • Fitts’s Law
            • Wayfinding
            Step 03 · PLP

            Filter without getting lost

            The listing page is where shoppers narrow the catalog down to one shoe. Five filters sit in one bar, each opens in place, and the active filters stay visible above the grid. Open any filter to try it.

            01

            Orientation and context

            Breadcrumb, title and description tell the shopper where they are, before any product appears.

            • Visibility of system status
            02

            Families as a shortcut

            Model cards at the top lead straight to the product. Recognizing a shoe is easier than remembering its name.

            • Recognition over recall
            03

            One pattern, five cuts

            Gender, type, brand, family and size share one component: counts beside each option, and options with no results are dimmed.

            • Hick’s Law
            • Jakob’s Law
            04

            Filters you can read and undo

            Every chip carries its full label and its own remove button. One “clear filters” goes back to the full catalog.

            • User control and freedom
            • Fitts’s Law
            Step 04 · PDP

            Everything to decide, in one screen

            The product page is where the shopper commits. The new layout puts price, size, shipping and trust around one buy button, then closes with a bundle, reviews and next steps. Scroll the page or jump to a section.

            01

            Buy area on one screen

            Gallery, price with installments, size buttons and two actions with clear hierarchy. Shipping and trust signals sit next to the buy button; details open on demand.

            • Price anchoring
            • Fitts’s Law
            • Progressive disclosure
            02

            Cross-sell without friction

            Two items that match, each with its own discount. The total shows what you save, and the button says what is missing: select the size of 2 items to continue.

            • Gain framing
            • Error prevention
            03

            Proof where doubt peaks

            A 4.8 rating with a bar per star, “fits as expected: 78%” and the size each reviewer bought. A recommendations row means no dead end.

            • Social proof
            • Reduce uncertainty
            04

            Built for the thumb

            On mobile, buy now and add to cart go full width. Size comes before the actions, with a size guide, then shipping, seals and reviews in sequence.

            • Fitts’s Law
            • Hick’s Law
